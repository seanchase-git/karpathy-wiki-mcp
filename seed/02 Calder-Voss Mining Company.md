# Calder-Voss Mining Company

Source: Ashdown, R., "Copper and Tin in the Harrow Valley" (Millbrook Press, 1962), chapter 3

## Founding

The Calder-Voss Mining Company was established in 1869 by two partners, Tobias Calder, a Tallow Bridge ironmonger with a talent for raising money, and Marek Voss, a Bohemian-born prospector who had found copper-bearing outcrops on the Mirren Fells. Their first lease covered the Dunmoor workings, which would remain the company's principal mine for the next sixty-five years. Within three years the partners had raised enough capital to employ about 200 men.

## The transport problem

The great obstacle to profit was the Dunmoor road, a rutted cart track that was impassable for weeks each winter. Ore was moved on pack ponies and later in wagons, at a cost that ate most of the margin. In 1871 Calder hired a Norwegian-trained engineer, Ingrid Halvorsen Pell, with a brief to find a cheaper way down the valley. Her answer was a narrow-gauge railway. The board approved it against the wishes of Voss, who feared the expense and who sold most of his shares in protest, though he remained a director until his death in 1888.

The Harrow Valley Railway, which opened in 1879, transformed the company's finances. The cost of moving a ton of ore to the Tallow Bridge smelter fell by roughly two thirds. The company owned the line outright until the 1920s and ran it as a department of the mine, with Pell as its first locomotive superintendent.

## Growth

Output peaked in the 1890s. After the Gannet Bank rack section replaced the Sable Gap switchback on the steepest part of the climb in 1891, heavier trains meant the mine could work lower-grade seams profitably. The company built a smelter at Tallow Bridge, rows of cottages for its workers, and a reading room that became the town's first public library. At its height Calder-Voss employed about 900 people, making it by far the largest employer in the valley.

## Pell's role

Pell remained closely tied to the company even after founding her own firm, Pell & Daughters, in 1880. The firm held the contract for maintaining mine machinery and locomotives, and Pell served as a consulting engineer to the board until her retirement in 1905.

## Decline

Falling metal prices after the First World War and the exhaustion of the richest seams hurt the company badly. Tobias Calder's son Edmund took over as chairman in 1921 but could not stem the losses. The Dunmoor Mine closed in 1934, and the company was wound up the following year. The smelter was demolished in 1938, and only its chimney remains.

## Legacy

The company's records were deposited with the Harrow Valley Historical Society in 1979. Many of the terraced streets of Tallow Bridge, including Voss Row and Calder Terrace, still carry the founders' names.
