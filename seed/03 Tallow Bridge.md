# Tallow Bridge

Source: Tallow Bridge Town Council heritage trail leaflet (2006)

## A river town

Tallow Bridge sits at the lower end of the Harrow Valley, where the River Harrow leaves the hills and the valley opens into farmland. The town takes its name from the stone bridge built there in the 1600s, said to have been paid for by a family of candle makers. For two centuries it was a modest market town of perhaps 900 people, known for sheep fairs and a small tannery.

## The mining boom

Everything changed with the arrival of the Calder-Voss Mining Company. Tobias Calder, who lived above his ironmonger's shop on Bridge Street, helped to found the company in 1869, and the town became its commercial base. The company built a smelter on the riverside meadows and terraced housing on the slopes above. By 1891 the population had grown to more than 4,000. Voss Row and Calder Terrace, laid out in the 1870s, are still lived in today.

The Harrow Valley Railway gave the town its second great industry. Tallow Bridge was the lower terminus, and the station and goods yard filled the flat ground beside the river. Next to the yard stood the works of Pell & Daughters, the engineering firm founded by Ingrid Halvorsen Pell, who made her home on Tanner's Hill above the town. At its height the works employed 150 men and women, and it built and repaired locomotives for several smaller railways across the region.

## Civic life

The town's public library, established by the mining company as a reading room, opened in the 1880s and still occupies the same building on Bridge Street. Pell is commemorated by a bronze plaque at the station and by the Pell Memorial Hall, built in 1914 with money raised by public subscription. The hall now serves as the town's theatre.

## Decline and revival

The closure of the Dunmoor Mine in 1934 was a heavy blow. The smelter shut, families moved away, and by 1951, the year the railway closed, the population had fallen to about 2,300. The smelter was demolished in the 1930s, leaving only its chimney, which is now a protected landmark.

Revival began with the Harrow Valley Railway Preservation Society, founded in 1978. When the heritage railway reopened in 1986, Tallow Bridge became a destination for visitors, and tourism is now the town's largest source of income. The former goods yard houses a small museum of the valley's industrial history.

## Heritage trail

The council's trail follows a two-mile loop through the town. Stops include the station, the chimney of the old smelter, Voss Row, the Pell plaque, and the preserved locomotive "Mirren", displayed under cover at the heritage station. Leaflets recommend visiting on a steam day, when the lower six miles of the line are in operation.
