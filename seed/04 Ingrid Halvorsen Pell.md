# Ingrid Halvorsen Pell

Source: Entry in the Northern Engineers' Biographical Register (1931 edition)

## Early life

Ingrid Halvorsen Pell was born in Bergen, Norway, in 1841, the daughter of a ship's carpenter. She trained as a surveyor and civil engineer, working on mountain roads and small railways in western Norway before moving to England in 1868 following her marriage to the mechanical engineer Henry Pell. Henry died in 1875, and she kept his surname for the rest of her career.

## The Harrow Valley years

In 1871 Tobias Calder of the Calder-Voss Mining Company recruited her to solve the problem of moving ore from the Dunmoor Mine down the Harrow Valley. She moved to Tallow Bridge, surveyed a route in the course of a single wet winter, and proposed a narrow-gauge railway of 2 ft 6 in, a decision she defended before a sceptical board. Her chief technical contribution was the switchback grade at Sable Gap, which allowed the Harrow Valley Railway to climb a wall of rock that other engineers had judged impassable for anything but a rope-worked incline. Construction began in 1872 and was completed under her supervision, with the line running 19 miles from Tallow Bridge to Dunmoor.

She became the company's first locomotive superintendent. In 1880 she left day-to-day employment to found her own firm, Pell & Daughters, at Tallow Bridge. The name referred to her two daughters, Astrid and Liv, both trained engineers, who joined her in the business. The firm built and repaired locomotives, designed winding gear for the Dunmoor shafts, and took contracts on other narrow-gauge lines.

## The Pell rack

Pell's best-known design came in 1891. The Sable Gap switchback had become a bottleneck, and the steepest part of the climb, Gannet Bank at 1-in-6, was replaced by a rack railway section using a toothed centre rail. Her adaptation of the rack system, with a simplified cog wheel that could be fitted to existing locomotives, was later copied on two narrow-gauge lines in Wales and one in Norway. Engineers still refer to this arrangement as the "Pell rack".

## Later years

Pell continued as a consulting engineer to the Calder-Voss board until 1905, when she retired. She lived on Tanner's Hill in Tallow Bridge and died there in 1912, aged seventy-one. A bronze plaque at the station records her work, and the Pell Memorial Hall, opened in 1914, was built by public subscription in her honour. Astrid Pell ran the firm until it closed in 1948.

## Assessment

The register describes her as "among the most practical engineers the northern valleys produced", noting that her insistence on narrow gauge made the Harrow Valley Railway affordable when a standard-gauge scheme would have bankrupted its backers. Her switchback grade at Sable Gap and her rack railway at Gannet Bank remain the standard examples of both techniques in the region.
